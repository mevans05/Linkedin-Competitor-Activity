## Who we are

Zilker Trail Consulting (getzilker.com) — a digital growth consultancy for
commerce, spanning **Marketing, Sales, and Digital Product** under one
method. Scale: 100+ team members, 20+ years in digital, clients across 6
countries / 15 US states, B2C and B2B.

**Tagline**: *"Win Bigger. Ship Faster."*

**Flagship offer**: **Zilker Trail Pathfinder** — "Your AI-enabled Growth
Engine." A modern product-development lifecycle (PDLC) for commerce growth,
"super-charged by our proprietary expertise, process, technology and AI,"
with named AI-acceleration use cases at each of 7 stages (Discover & Define
→ Requirements & Specs → Design & UX → Engineering → Test & Validation →
Release & Optimize → Measure & Evolve).

## Our method: GPS (first-principles, not frameworks-for-their-own-sake)

**Goals → Problems → Solution Hypotheses → Build & Launch → Outcomes &
Storytelling.** The operating thesis: *"If I only had 60 minutes to save the
world, I'd spend 55 minutes understanding the problem and only 5 minutes
finding the solution."* (Einstein, as quoted in our own deck — this is the
lens we apply to client work: problem definition over solutioning.)

Solution hypotheses are framed explicitly as falsifiable bets: *"I believe
that… If I am right… this will be the result."* — build, test, and learn in
the leanest way possible, then measure and let outcomes drive the next
cycle.

## How we differentiate — stated directly against named competitor categories

Slide 13 of our own deck ("It's Hard to Find Great Outside Help") names and
critiques the competitive landscape directly — this is the most useful
material for weekly POV-writing, since it's already our own articulated
disagreement:

- **"AI consulting 'top 100 prompts'"** firms — critiqued as "very limited"
  on execution, growth strategy, ecosystem knowledge, change management,
  test & learn, and enablement.
- **AI-native agencies**, **platform-focused SIs** (this is where Accenture,
  Deloitte, KPMG, PwC, McKinsey, and Insight Enterprises sit from our
  perspective), **media agencies**, and **"no humans needed" AI startups** —
  all named as structurally distinct failure modes.
- **Our conclusion, verbatim**: *"Current options: Structurally aren't
  geared to deliver consistent growth."* And: *"Still too many ambulance
  chasers in these early days of change."*

**Our positive claim** (repeated as the core POV on two slides): *"Consistent
growth requires a unified, orchestrated system"* — "smartly enabled by AI &
automation," not delivered by siloed point solutions. We explicitly name
**silos** (People, Process, Data, Tech, Content, Culture) as the thing that
"hinders the ability to evolve and deliver growth" — a direct contrast to
generalist consultancies that hand off between siloed teams/phases.

**On delivery speed**: *"The build process is broken."* *"Time to Value is
FAR too long — the current model is bloated, inefficient and far too slow"*
— citing an industry-average of 100+ days across the standard 7-stage PDLC.
This is a recurring, quantified critique we're positioned to make of any
competitor content about "transformation" or multi-year change programs.

## Our AI POV — pragmatic and specifically anti-hype, not anti-AI

From the AI Philosophy doc: *"AI that earns its place. Pragmatic.
ROI-obsessed. Tool-agnostic."*

- **"ROI is Non-Negotiable"** — *"Every AI initiative... must define upfront
  what success looks like in business terms... If we cannot measure it, we
  do not scale it."*
- **"Traditional Tooling Default"** — deterministic tools (rules engines,
  APIs, low-code) are the default for process automation; AI is "reserved
  for the uncertain, generative, or insight-heavy layers," not applied by
  default.
- **Effectiveness over novelty**: *"A simple script that reliably saves 10
  hours/week beats a flashy agent that saves 2 hours unreliably."*
- **We have a specific, data-quantified skepticism of AI adoption hype** —
  our own deck argues *"AI Adoption is Often a Myth"*: broad-based adoption
  stats mask that only a small handful are using AI to deliver real value.
  We cite a specific breakdown: while "the dashboard" reports ~80% adoption,
  the reality is 1–5% have an agent in production, 10% have written
  skills/MD files, and 65% have only prompted a bot fewer than 5 times (or
  never opened it). **This is a ready-made, specific counter to any
  competitor's generic "AI adoption is accelerating" claim** — we don't deny
  AI's value, we dispute the adoption numbers being used to sell it.
- **Rollout philosophy**: don't mandate AI broadly — identify a pilot cohort
  of "Willing High-Performers" (high skill + high willingness), prove value
  there first, and "push AI to the background for the rest." Explicitly
  *not* "everyone must adopt AI now."
- **Local-first / right-sized**: prefer local models (Ollama or equivalent)
  for internal tooling and latency-sensitive work; escalate to larger cloud
  models "only when the task genuinely requires it and the ROI justifies the
  cost and latency." Avoid vendor lock-in; design exit paths from day one.
- **Values this serves**: Disciplined Excellence, Servant Leadership,
  Truth-telling, Pursuit of Craft, Driven to Win — "AI must serve those
  values, never undermine them." "No sacred cows": retire capabilities that
  stop clearing the ROI bar, "sentiment or sunk cost does not dictate our
  technology choices."

## Voice

Direct, quantified, and comfortable naming a specific failure mode rather
than gesturing at one generally ("Average > 100 days," "1–5% have an agent
in production" — not "adoption is slower than reported"). Confident enough
to critique named categories of competitor outright ("ambulance chasers,"
"structurally aren't geared to deliver"). Anti-hype without being anti-AI —
the recurring move is *pragmatic and specific* vs. a competitor's *broad and
aspirational*. Comfortable using a borrowed quote (Einstein) when it sharpens
a point, and comfortable saying "we build for the level each team is at, not
the level the slide is at."

## Topics we have a strong, specific POV on

- AI adoption reality vs. hype/dashboard stats (our sharpest, most
  differentiated topic — quantified skepticism, not blanket dismissal)
- PDLC / time-to-value speed in digital product and commerce delivery
- Org silos as the actual blocker to growth (not tooling)
- Pilot-first, power-user-first AI rollout vs. broad mandates
- Problem-definition rigor (GPS method) vs. solution-first thinking
- Where "transformation" partners are structurally mismatched to the work
  (named categories above)

## Topics to be cautious or silent on

- Deep domain content clearly outside commerce/marketing/sales/digital
  product (e.g., tax policy, audit, industry-specific regulatory content)
  unless it intersects growth or AI adoption.
- Anything that would read as anti-AI rather than anti-hype — the brand
  position is "AI that earns its place," not AI skepticism.
